# The daily check-in

Numbers from your watch only tell part of the story. The check-in is where you record how the day actually felt, so you can see it next to your training later.

## Filling it in

Open **Journal**, pick a day, and scroll to **Check-in**. Or use **Add a check-in** on the dashboard, which takes you straight to the latest day.

Everything is optional. If you didn't notice something, leave it empty. Empty is saved as "not recorded", not as a low score or a no, so a skipped answer never makes a day look worse than it was.

Each account starts with three fields:

- **Energy**, from 1 to 5. 1 is "Running on empty, hard to get through the day" and 5 is "Great, full of energy".
- **Soreness**, from 1 to 5. 1 is "None" and 5 is "Severe, it stopped me".
- **Notes**, for tags (like *heat* or *travel*) and a short note of up to 240 characters. Tags you've used before show up as buttons so you can tap them again.

Tap a number to pick it, and tap it again to clear it. The description for that number shows underneath, so you don't have to remember what a 3 means.

Press **Save check-in**. You can come back and change it any time, and the button then says **Save changes**. To wipe a day's answers, open **Clear this day**, tick the box and press **Clear day**.

If you have the journal open in two tabs, Baseline+ won't let an older tab overwrite something you saved in the newer one. Reload and try again.

## Questions from your metrics

When you check in on today or yesterday, a short question can appear above a field if your HRV, resting heart rate or last night's sleep was outside your usual range, higher or lower. For example: "HRV is lower than usual (38 ms, usual 45 to 62). Is your energy lower than usual today?"

- Your usual range is the middle 80% of your own readings from the 28 days before, and it needs at least 14 of them. With less history, or no reading for the day itself, there's no question.
- You'll see two at most, the biggest change first.
- **Why?** shows the day's value, your range and the last few weeks as a small chart.
- The question goes away once you answer the field, or tap × to dismiss it for that day.
- It's only ever a question. It never fills anything in or tells you what to do.

HRV and resting heart rate come from your Apple Health import. Sleep comes from Apple Health sleep tracking, not from rest minutes you typed in.

## Setting it up for you

Go to **Settings > Daily check-in**.

**When do you usually check in?** Choose evening (about the day you just had) or morning (about last night and today). Evening is the default.

**Fields on your check-in.** You can rename fields, move them up or down, and hide ones you don't use. To add your own, give it a name and pick a type:

- **Score 1 to 5** for things like dizziness, pain or brain fog.
- **Yes / no** for things like "took my rescue medication" or "had a flare".
- **Number**, with an optional unit, for things like fluids in litres or salt in grams.

**POTS starter set.** If you have POTS, or something like it, the starter set adds Dizziness, Brain fog, Flare today, Fluids (L), Salt (g), Compression worn and Meds taken in one go. Anything you already track under the same name is left alone, and a field you'd hidden comes back instead of being added twice. Rename, hide or reorder them afterwards like any other field. The box disappears once you have every field in the set.

Hiding a field takes it off the form but keeps everything you've already recorded. You'll find it under **Hidden fields**, where you can bring it back. You can also delete a field you added yourself, but that removes all of its answers too, so you'll be asked to confirm.

## Fixing a day's numbers

The same Journal page has **Daily totals** above the check-in. You can type in a day's steps, move, rest and breathwork by hand, or correct numbers that came from an import. Leave a total blank if it wasn't recorded. Corrected days are marked as manually corrected, and the original source is kept.

Days that include individual activities show their totals but can't be edited one number at a time.
