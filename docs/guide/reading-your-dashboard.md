# Reading your dashboard

The dashboard always shows your most recent recorded day, and compares it with your own history. It never compares you with anyone else.

## Readiness, at the top

The big number is your readiness score for that day, out of 100, if one was recorded. The headline is just a label for that number:

- 70 or more: **Ready to train**
- 50 to 69: **Take it steady**
- Under 50: **Recovery day**

Underneath, it tells you how far the score is above or below your usual, meaning the middle value of the 28 days before. It needs at least four scored days to work that out.

Baseline+ doesn't calculate readiness itself. The score has to come from your data, for example a spreadsheet import from a service that gives you one. Apple Health doesn't have a readiness score, so if that's your only source you'll see "No readiness score today", and everything else still works.

These labels describe a number. They aren't advice about whether you should train. You know your body and your condition, and your care team knows your situation.

On the right you'll see that day's energy and soreness from your check-in, with buttons to add or edit the check-in and to import data.

## The four cards

Steps, move (active calories), rest (time asleep, when it comes from Apple Health) and breathwork (mindful minutes) for the day. Under each one is the percentage difference from your usual for that measure, again the middle value of the 28 days before. Until you have at least four recorded days in that window, the card says there aren't enough days for a baseline yet.

Tap a card to see that measure's history and, where it's available, what made up each day's total.

## Training chart

The chart shows your training minutes each day, stacked by sport: running, cycling, swimming, yoga and strength. Choose 2 weeks, 6 weeks or 3 months above it.

- The line across the top is your readiness score, read against the right-hand scale.
- Shaded days are days you rated your energy 2 or lower.

That combination is the point of the chart. You can see whether a heavy few days came before a run of low energy, or whether a rest week helped.

The sport comes from the activity's name, so walks and activities it doesn't recognise aren't on the chart. They still count toward your daily totals.

## Recent

Your latest sessions, newest first, with how long each one was.

## Comparisons

A table with each measure's latest value, how much it changed from the previous recorded day, and how far it is from your average across all of your history.

## Blank days and gaps

A day you didn't record is a gap, not a zero. Blank totals are left out of every average and comparison, so a forgotten watch doesn't drag your numbers down. A real zero, like a day with no breathwork, is counted as zero.

A pattern you notice here is a pattern in your records. It doesn't prove one thing caused another. Still, it's often a useful thing to bring to an appointment.
