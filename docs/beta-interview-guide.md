# Beta tester profile and interview guide

## Purpose

Learn how people currently make sense of activity and recovery information, where that process is confusing, and what data they can practically provide. These interviews inform the first audience and intake path; they do not validate a medical or readiness claim.

## Tester profile

Recruit people who:

- Do a regular sport, fitness, or other physical activity and sometimes use recovery information to plan what to do next.
- Have recently looked at activity, sleep, rest, or readiness information from an app, wearable, or their own notes.
- Can describe a real example of a confusing or hard-to-interpret day, even if they do not use a dedicated recovery score.

Include a mix of people who track consistently and people who track only some measures. Do not screen on diagnosis, treatment, or other sensitive health details. Ask about device and data access only as needed to understand a possible intake path; participation must not depend on sharing an export.

## Before the interview

- Plan for about 25 minutes. Explain that the goal is to learn about their current process, not to evaluate them or sell a product.
- Ask permission before taking notes or recording. If recording is declined, take brief notes without names.
- Do not request account credentials, screenshots containing personal details, or identifiable health records. A participant may describe an export or use a synthetic/redacted sample instead.
- Let the participant skip any question or stop at any time. Remove names and identifying details from notes.

## Interview questions

Start with the participant's experience. Do not introduce Baseline or suggest a preferred answer until the closing questions.

1. **Recent confusing day:** “Think of a recent day when it was hard to decide how recovered or ready you felt. What was going on?”
   - What information did you look at, and what did you do next?
   - What part was clear, and what part was confusing?
2. **Current tools:** “Where do you usually look for information about your activity or recovery?”
   - Which devices, apps, or personal notes do you use?
   - Do you use them regularly or only in particular situations?
3. **Making sense of measures:** “When different measures or apps seem to disagree, how do you work out what to trust?”
   - Can you recall a recent example?
   - What context, if any, would have helped explain the difference?
4. **Current workaround:** “Have you found a way to bring this information together or answer the question yourself?”
   - What steps does that take, and how often do you do it?
   - What have you tried and stopped using?
5. **Data access:** “If you wanted to review past information in another tool, what could you access or export?”
   - Which source would be easiest to start with?
   - Is the relevant device yours, shared, or borrowed, and do you control the account that holds its data?
   - What makes exporting or sharing data difficult, uncomfortable, or not worthwhile?
   - Do not ask them to sign in, connect an account, or hand over a file during the interview.
6. **Useful answer:** “What would a useful answer to your original question have included?”
   - Would a comparison, a time trend, or context from another source help? Why?
   - What would make the answer believable or unhelpful?
7. **Close:** Briefly describe the idea of seeing a person's activity and recovery measures together with their dates and sources visible. “What would you expect this to help with? What would you not want it to tell you or do?”
   - What would you need to understand before trying it?
   - Is there anything important about your current process that I missed?
